Llama Score User Manual
  1. Download the Llama Score Dashboard (the button at the top of this page). It comes as a .zip - unzip the whole thing into a permanent folder first (e.g. your Desktop or Documents), not left inside Downloads or a still-zipped view. Everything in that folder needs to stay together and stay put, since it's where the Dashboard will also keep its recorded ledger.
  2. Run Llama Score Dashboard.exe from that folder and leave it running in the background while you play (right-click the exe → "Send to" → "Desktop (create shortcut)" if you want it easy to relaunch next time). Its Settings button controls two folders, both already defaulted for a typical install:
    • EU5 save folder - where it watches for new autosaves. Defaults to your own Documents\Paradox Interactive\Europa Universalis V\save games. Only change this if your saves live somewhere non-default (a different drive, a custom Documents location, etc).
    • Ledger data folder - where it records what it sees. Defaults to a data folder it creates right next to the .exe - this is the same folder you'll point this webpage at in the next step. Only change this if you specifically want the ledger stored somewhere else; most people never need to touch it.
  3. Play normally. EU5 autosaves periodically; the Dashboard reads each new one as it lands and builds up a running log of every war in your campaign - including wars that get cleared out of the save itself before you'd otherwise catch them as "concluded."
  4. Come back to this tab any time you want to check scores. Click "Connect campaign folder…" below (one-time per browser) and pick the data folder that appeared next to Llama Score Dashboard.exe once you ran it - after that, loading any save from a linked campaign shows its Llama Score automatically, no more picking required.
  5. Multiplayer note: there's no server and no automatic syncing between different players' computers - each person who wants to see live scores runs their own copy of the Dashboard against their own save folder. Since everyone's client is autosaving the same shared campaign, everyone ends up tracking the same wars independently.

Manual setup / troubleshooting
ⓘ ✅ Linked!

Pick the recorder's data folder — next to Llama Score Dashboard.exe if you used the Dashboard app, or directly inside llama-score-automatic-logging-machine if you ran the CLI recorder script. Don't pick its parent folder, and not one specific campaign's folder inside data/campaigns.

How are these scores calculated?
GP Score
(27 − Great Power Rank + 1) × 75, floored at 0 for rank 28+. Rank 1 (strongest) scores highest; only the top 27 ranked countries score anything.
War Score
Per finished war: reward for winning against more Enemies with fewer Allies to share credit with; penalty for losing scaled the same way. A war with no real combatant on either side (a hired condottieri contract) uses a flat ±2 instead. In PVE mode, a vassal/subject fighting alongside its own overlord on the same side doesn't count as a separate Enemy or Ally - beating "the Ottomans + 3 vassals" scores the same as beating just "the Ottomans", since it's really one fight against one empire. A vassal fighting entirely on its own (its overlord isn't in that war) still counts normally.
How a winner is decided
EU5 clears the in-game war score once a war concludes, so nothing in the save directly says who won. Only three things are ever trusted to call a winner, in this order: an enforced war reparations payment (read straight from the peace treaty - the payer lost, the receiver won), a war whose goal was literally independence (decided by whether the vassal is still subject to its former overlord afterward), or a real land transfer between the war's two original sides (their territory measurably shrank/grew - not land merely occupied mid-war, which doesn't mean it was kept). Everything else - the in-game war score, battle losses, who's occupying what when the war disappears, treasury swing, prestige swing - is shown as a "considered but not decisive" factor on that war's row, never used to pick a side, because each one produced real false positives on tested campaign data even when it looked one-sided.
Llama Points / Alpaca Points
(GP Score ÷ 100) + ∑ War Score across every one of the player's scored wars this campaign - called Llama Points in PVP mode and Alpaca Points in PVE mode so the two leaderboards are never confused for one another, even though it's the exact same formula either way.
White Peace
When none of the three decisive signals above fire - no reparations, no independence, no territory actually changing hands between the war's original sides - the war is scored as a flat 0 for both sides rather than guessing, shown separately below from wars that were actually decided. This is the expected result for a war fought mostly through allies/vassals with nothing conclusive between the two original combatants, not a sign the algorithm gave up - the "How decided" column's tooltip still shows what it considered, and a manual override in "Edit war corrections" fixes any case where the real outcome was clearer in-game than the data shows.
Which wars come out clearest
A clean conquest (one side's territory measurably shrinks, the other's grows) is the single most reliable case. An indemnity peace or an independence war is just as reliable and needs no territory to change hands at all, since both are read from a direct peace-treaty fact rather than inferred. A war that ends with nothing above resolving between the two original sides - fought entirely through allies, or genuinely inconclusive - falls to White Peace by design, even if the in-game war score or battle losses "look" one-sided.
Single save vs. a connected campaign ledger
Loading just one save with no Dashboard/recorder data connected gives the signals above nothing to compare against - there's no "before the war" snapshot to diff. In that mode, scoring falls back to a much rougher guess (whichever side is occupying more of the war's contested land right now), so treat those numbers as an estimate. Connecting the Llama Score Dashboard's campaign folder is what unlocks the real detection above - the recorder snapshots each war right before and after it disappears from the save, which is exactly the before/after comparison reparations, independence, and land transfer need, and it catches wars EU5 purges from the save before your next autosave would otherwise have caught them as concluded.
Why is a war greyed out / marked "auto"?
A war row is automatically excluded from scoring (an auto badge - hover it for the exact reason) rather than counted, when:
  • Not a PvP result - the opposing side was never player-controlled.
  • Not a PvE result (PVE mode only) - the opposing side had a real player.
  • Player had already left - this player's country had already reverted to AI before the war even began. A war they were actually playing when it started still counts, even if they left partway through it.
  • Opponent had already left - every enemy in the war had already left the campaign before it even began. A war fought against a real opponent still counts even if that opponent left partway through it.
  • Never actually fought (PvP only) - joined the war but never recorded a Battle/Capture loss.
  • Manually hidden - this player was hidden as departed on the Players table.
Every one of these can be overridden per-war in "Edit war corrections" below if the automatic read is wrong for a specific war.
